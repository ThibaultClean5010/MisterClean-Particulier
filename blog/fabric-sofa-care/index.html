Fabric sofa care

How to care for a fabric sofa between professional cleans

Your sofa is part of everyday life. Looking after it does not need to mean scrubbing harder or trying every stain-removal trick you see online.

By MisterClean Services10 September 20264 min read

In this article

  1. Check the care label
  2. Keep everyday care gentle
  3. Respond to spills
  4. Know your covers
  5. When to ask for help

The first rule: follow the care instructions for your particular sofa and fabric. A method that suits one cover may be unsuitable for another.

1. Check the care label before reaching for a cleaner

A fabric name alone is not a cleaning instruction. Find the label or the manufacturer's care guide for your sofa, including any separate instructions for its covers. Keep a photo of those instructions somewhere easy to find, so you are not searching for them while dealing with a spill.

Before using an approved stain remover, test it on a hidden area as directed. If you cannot identify the fabric or find its care instructions, ask the retailer or a professional before applying a product. IKEA's sofa care guidance also recommends testing stain removers out of sight first.

Reference: IKEA Australia — fabric sofa care, page 5 (PDF).

2. Keep everyday care gentle

Where your sofa's instructions permit it, vacuum gently with low suction and a soft upholstery attachment. Give the seats, arms and headrests attention rather than waiting for obvious marks. KING recommends weekly vacuuming for its fabrics; use your own manufacturer's guidance as the starting point for your routine.

Think about where the sofa sits, too. Freedom recommends protecting indoor furniture from direct sunlight, including sunlight through windows. Curtains or blinds can help. Rotate interchangeable cushions where permitted to share out everyday wear, rather than always using the same seat.

References: KING — fabric care and Freedom — furniture care.

3. Absorb a spill without scrubbing

For a fresh liquid spill, begin with a clean, absorbent cloth and let it take up the liquid. Do not rub the area or soak the upholstery. Follow the fabric-specific instructions for any next step; stopping to check is better than layering several products onto the same mark.

KING warns that unsuitable cleaning products, vigorous rubbing and incorrect spot or steam cleaning can damage the appearance of upholstery. Avoid treating a household cleaning recipe as universally safe. If a mark remains, note what spilled and anything you have already applied before asking for advice.

Reference: KING — fabric products, spills and cleaning methods.

4. A zip does not automatically mean machine-washable

Removable covers and washable covers are not the same thing. Koala, for example, explains that some sofa covers can be removed but should remain in place for care, while particular models have machine-washable outer covers. The model and cover instructions matter.

If washing is permitted, follow the stated washing and drying directions rather than choosing a hotter setting for a stubborn stain. Keep the instructions for the seat covers and the main sofa cover together: they may not be identical.

Reference: Koala — removable sofa covers.

5. Know when to ask for a professional assessment

If the care label calls for professional cleaning, you are unsure about a treatment, or a stain is still visible, ask for an assessment. For your enquiry, take a clear photo of the whole sofa, a close-up of the mark and a photo of the care label. Mention the cause and age of the stain if you know them.
